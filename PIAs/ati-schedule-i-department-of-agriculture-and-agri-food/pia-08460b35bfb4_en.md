# Prescribed Presence in the Workplace Monitoring Program Privacy Impact Assessment

## Description

The Treasury Board Secretariat's (TBS) [Direction on prescribed presence in the workplace](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/publicservice/workforce/direction-prescribed-presence-workplace.html) (TBS Direction), issued May 1, 2024, and accompanying [Message to Deputy Ministers: Updates to the hybrid work model in the public service](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/publicservice/hybrid-work/hybrid-work-timeline/message-updates-hybrid-work-model.html) mandate that non-executive (non-EX) employees work in-office three days per week (or 60% of their schedule), and EX employees work in-office four days per week (or 80% of their schedule). Changes to this direction were released in February 2026, which will require EX employees to be in the office full-time as of May 4, 2026, and the intention for non-EX employees to increase their presence in the office to at least four days per week as of July 6, 2026.

Per the TBS Direction, to monitor employee compliance, aggregate reports can be created using personal information through the use of turnstile data, existing attendance reports, and/or Internet Protocol (IP) login data. However, the reports must be aggregate and not identify employees and data associated with the number of days they were in the office and/or teleworking.

Based on the TBS Direction and guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner — specifically the Office's [July 16, 2024 Privacy Act bulletin](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/for-federal-institutions/privacy-act-bulletins/pab_20240716/) — a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) is required to support the collection and use of reports, which support proactive monitoring. Under this new Workplace Monitoring Program, AAFC will create network connectivity reports of identifiable employees, which can be used to support proactive monitoring of employees with the TBS Direction.

Historically at AAFC, the Human Resources Branch and Information Systems Branch have used existing AAFC data sets to create aggregate reports on employee network connectivity to report branch level statistics on compliance with the in-office requirement. However, over time, the data has shown to be ineffective in assessing employee-level compliance with the TBS Direction; therefore, employee level data/reports are necessary.

Beginning on or around July 2026, AAFC will begin providing management, Labour Relations, and branch planning employees network connectivity data for each employee. The personalized reports of a manager's direct and indirect subordinates will be produced to show the days that an employee connected their AAFC laptop to the Virtual Private Network (VPN) (a day teleworking) and the days that an employee connected to the in-house Government of Canada network (a day in the office). This new personalized report will assist management in better understanding employee compliance with the TBS Direction.

To produce these reports/dashboards within Power BI, AAFC will only use data from PeopleSoft, Microsoft 365 (Entra ID and Microsoft Defender), and a few other HR data sets, but excluding data from GCcoworking locations and access card records.

The personalized connectivity reports may be used in an HR related activity related to employee non-adherence, including awareness, training, attendance management, and performance management. In cases of ongoing and persistent non-adherence, the reports may also be considered — following consultation with Labour Relations — as part of progressive disciplinary measures up to and including termination of employment.

Human Resources Branch's current aggregate reports to assistant deputy ministers and branch planning staff will continue to be created as they have in the past.

Human Resources Branch is developing communication products to inform staff and management of the new program.

## Why a Privacy Impact Assessment was completed

According to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner's [July 16, 2024 Privacy Act bulletin](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/for-federal-institutions/privacy-act-bulletins/pab_20240716/), a PIA is required when a federal institution intends on conducting proactive monitoring. Regarding TBS policy, Appendix C.2.2.9.2.1 of the TBS Directive on Privacy Practices states a PIA is required, in part, when any new or modified information technology (IT) or other process is being deployed. As the new program involves a new IT solution to produce personalized network connectivity reports, which may be used by management in employee discipline, this PIA is required.

## Additional information

The PIA identified privacy risks associated with the administration of the program, including issues around accountability, transparency, and information security. In response to the risks, senior management within Human Resources Branch developed an action plan to mitigate the risks within a timely fashion.

## Related personal information banks

The institution specific personal information bank, Prescribed Presence in the Workplace Monitoring Program (AAFC PPE 254), was created during the writing of this PIA. AAFC has published the PIB on its Info Source webpage while it is being reviewed, approved, and registered by TBS.
