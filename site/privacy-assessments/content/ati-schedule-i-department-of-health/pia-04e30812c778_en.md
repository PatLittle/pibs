# Biocides Compliance and Enforcement (C&E) Program

## Description

The Biocides Compliance and Enforcement (C&E) Program oversees the activities of regulated parties under the *Food and Drug Ac*t. Its purpose is to address compliance issues with marketed biocide products. Potential issues may be identified through consumer or industry complaints, referrals from provincial or federal partners, international counterparts, or through Health Canada's (HC) compliance monitoring activities. Concerns may include matters related to product ingredients, labelling, unauthorized sale, advertising, or risks to public health and safety.

These activities help ensure that only safe, effective, and authorized biocides are sold in Canada. Individuals or organizations may submit complaints or questions to the Program which may include personal information. The information provided, along with other relevant data, is utilized to respond to questions and to assess compliance and identify potential C&E actions.

## Why a privacy impact assessment was completed

As per Section C.2.2.9 of the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat (TBS) Directive on Privacy Practices, the Biocides C&E program meets the following criteria to initiate a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA):

- When an existing program or activity that uses personal information for an administrative purpose does not already have a Personal Information Bank (PIB).

The PIA identifies and assesses privacy risks to personal information collected and used by the Biocides C&E program which covers the receipt and triage of biocide-related complaints/referrals and provides company notifications of non-compliance or risk issues, as well as the subsequent C&E actions carried out under the *Food and Drugs Act* and Biocides Regulations. The scope begins when:

1. a complaint or referral is received; or
2. a company notification is submitted to the Health Products Compliance and Risk Management Division (HPCRM).

The scope ends once the complaint or notification has been screened, triaged, and either actioned by HPCRM, closed, or referred to the appropriate HC program area, or other domestic or foreign regulatory groups for further action.

## Additional information

This PIA examines the collection, use, retention, disclosure, and disposal of personal information by HC during the intake and triage of biocide-related complaints and inquiries, in accordance with the *Food and Drug Act*.

The assessment identified several areas where privacy practices can be strengthened. The Program is actively working to address these areas as part of its ongoing efforts to enhance privacy protections:

- **Information Sharing Agreements and Disclosure Management**
  - The Program is developing formal agreements with recipient programs and organizations, including other HC programs and government departments, to govern the sharing of personal information. For one-time disclosures, HPCRM will collaborate with the Privacy Management Division (PMD) to develop language defining permitted uses. This will enhance the safeguarding of shared personal information and improve documentation and oversight of disclosures.
- **Data Minimization, Retention, Disposal and Communication**
  - The Program is working with its Branch Information Management Advisor to establish a records retention and disposition schedule aligned with Library and Archives Canada standards. To support data minimization, HPCRM will revise complaint forms and explore options to guide individuals on what personal information is necessary to provide.
- **Access Controls and Auditing**
  - To address risks related to unauthorized access, HPCRM will implement a regular audit process to monitor access rights and ensure they are based on the need-to-know principle. This includes strengthening access controls and establishing centralized audit procedures.
- **Transparency and Individual Awareness**
  - To improve transparency, HPCRM and PMD will submit an amended PIB to the Treasury Board Secretariat in October 2025, ensuring it accurately describes the personal information collected and processed. HPCRM will also introduce clear instructions on complaint forms to help individuals understand what information is required.
