### Project description

The purposes of the ICA (which has been in place since 1985) are:

- To provide for the review of significant investments in Canada by non-Canadians for likely net benefit; and
- To provide for the review of investments in Canada by non-Canadians for potential national security injury.

Foreign investors subject to the Investment Canada Act (ICA), may submit the following:

- Notification to Acquire Control of an Existing Canadian Business (or Businesses);
- Notification to Establish a New Canadian Business;
- Application for Net Benefit Review; or
- Voluntary Notification of Investments in Paragraph 25.1(c).

In conducting ICA reviews, some personal information of individuals in connection to potential foreign investors—Investment Review Division (IRD) clients—is collected. In 2017, these information elements were expanded to include investors' residential contact information, and the dates of birth of certain Directors of Boards, in addition to other information detailed under section III of this PIA.

Pursuant to the TBS Directive on Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA), this full, core PIA is being conducted in response to 2017 changes to the personal information elements collected, which constituted a significant change to an existing program which collects and uses personal information (as detailed by the Directive). This PIA identifies, assesses and mitigates the risks to privacy associated with conducting ICA reviews.

## Section II – Risk area identification and categorization

The TBS requires that core PIAs include a completed risk identification and categorization section as outlined below. To have consistent risk categories and risk measurement across government institutions, standardized risk areas (itemized below) and a common risk scale are used as the basis for risk analysis.

The numbered risk scale is presented in an ascending order: the first level (1) represents the lowest level of potential risk for the given risk area; the fourth level (4) represents the highest level of potential risk for the given risk area.

| Considerations:  Potential foreign investors complete various forms that are used by ISED's IRD to initiate the review process under the ICA. ### H – Potential risk that in the event of a privacy breach, there will be an impact on an individual or employee.

Considerations:

- If personal information is compromised, it has a low risk of potential impact to the relevant individual(s), which would be limited to harm in the form of embarrassment. Such information might include a name with a date of birth and a business or residential address, and the fact that the individual is among their organization's highest-paid employees, as well as an individual's intent to invest in a Canadian business interest. It is not believed that this information has the potential to cause serious harm to any relevant individuals.
- If an ISED employee is directly responsible for compromising personal information, it has a low-to-medium potential impact to the relevant employee, which could include embarrassment, but might also include some form of consequential treatment commensurate with the employee's role in the breach (for example, an employee found responsible for deliberately breaching an individual candidate's privacy would be subject to disciplinary measures, up to and including dismissal, while an employee found to have inadvertently contributed to a breach of privacy might be required by their manager to attend in-depth departmental privacy training, etc.).
