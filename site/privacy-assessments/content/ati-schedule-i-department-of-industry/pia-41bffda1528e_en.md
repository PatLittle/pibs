# Transparency of Beneficial Ownership of Federally Incorporated Companies – Evergreen Update to Support Discrepancy Reporting Requirements

## Description

In Budget 2022, the Government committed to implement, by the end of 2023, a public and searchable registry of beneficial owners of federal corporations. Beneficial owners are the natural persons who, through direct or indirect means, have ultimate ownership or exercise control over a corporation. These are distinct from legal owners, who can be either a natural person or another legal person (such as another corporation or trust). The federal government’s decision to build a beneficial ownership registry arose from heightened international pressure to combat tax evasion, money laundering and terrorist financing in Canada as well as the federal, provincial and territorial governments’ commitments to take measures to reduce the use of Canadian corporations as vehicles for illegal activity. Illicit actors use a variety of means to attempt to obscure their beneficial ownership information, including through the use of shell companies, complex ownership structures, and other legal arrangements.

ISED was tasked with implementing this initiative. The goal is to provide greater access to information about who owns and controls Canadian businesses. Information on the true owners of companies is an essential part of a well-functioning economy and society. This information, known as beneficial ownership transparency, helps to deter the misuse of business corporations, helps domestic and foreign law enforcement agencies investigate fraudulent activities like money laundering and tax evasion, protects consumers, reduces investment risk and improves national and global governance.

A first set of amendments to the CBCA (via Budget Implementation Act, 2022, No. 1 – Division 30, sections 430-435) received Royal Assent on 23 June 2022. These amendments require federally incorporated companies to send information about their beneficial owners (defined in the CBCA as ‘individuals with significant control’ or ‘ISCs’) to Corporations Canada. These amendments also enable Corporations Canada to provide all or part of the information it receives about ISCs to an investigative body, FINTRAC or other prescribed entities.

A second set of amendments to the CBCA (via Bill C-42) sets out additional requirements, including the information about ISCs that is made available to the public and enables Corporations Canada to provide all or part of the information it receives about ISCs to a provincial corporate registry or a provincial government department or agency that is responsible for corporate law in the relevant jurisdiction.

Both sets of amendments to the CBCA came into force on order in council on 22 January 2024.

Discrepancy Reporting:

The usefulness of the federal beneficial ownership registry depends on the accuracy of the information it contains. To strengthen corporate beneficial ownership transparency, the Proceeds of Crime, Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Regulations (PCMLTFR) were amended to require persons or entities regulated by FINTRAC (reporting entity) to report material discrepancies identified between the beneficial ownership information that they have obtained and the individuals-with-significant-control information available in Corporations Canada's database. This is required when the reporting entity determines that a corporation incorporated under the CBCA poses a high risk of a money laundering offence or terrorist activity financing offence. In the case of a CBCA corporation that does not pose a high risk of a money laundering offence or terrorist activity financing offence, but for which a reporting entity identifies a material discrepancy, the reporting entity can voluntarily report a material discrepancy to Corporations Canada.

The amendments to the PCMLTFR came into force on 1 October 2025.

## Why a privacy impact assessment was completed

To support amendments to the *Proceeds of Crime, Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Regulations* that came into force on 1 October 2025.

## Additional information

No risks to privacy were identified that warrant further exploration or the development of risk mitigation strategies.

In this regard it is important to note the following rationale:

- This activity represents an enhancement to a years-old existing activity. Corporations Canada has long been in the business of collecting information about individuals who are directors of federally incorporated companies, while making some of that information publicly available, retaining the balance in confidence, and disclosing relevant information to investigative bodies—such as the RCMP—when necessary.
- The IT solution that supports this activity has long-been proven to be effective and secure, and the minor enhancements that will be made to support beneficial ownership transparency will be fully compliant with all Government of Canada IT security requirements.
- The new collections of personal information represent non-sensitive elements, many of which are required by law to be placed in the public domain.
- Due to the insensitive nature of those personal information elements that are not to be placed in the public domain, any inadvertent breach of privacy—the risk of which is deemed very low due to long-established methodologies—has the potential to cause only negligible harm to individuals, and none to ISED employees.
- ISED will conduct PIA “evergreen” updates within a reasonable period of time, commensurate with any changes to program operations.
