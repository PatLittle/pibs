# Reporting of Suspicious Transactions for Controlled Substances and Precursor Chemicals

## Description

In accordance with the *Controlled Drugs and Substances Act (CDSA)* and its associated regulations, Health Canada (HC) collects personal information from licensed and registered dealers as part of the suspicious transaction reporting process. Controlled substance licensed dealers are legally required to report any suspicious transactions involving controlled substances to the Minister of Health (Health Canada) within 72 hours of becoming aware of them. Precursor chemical licensed and registered dealers may also provide this information to HC.

HC uses this information to help detect or identify possible diversion of controlled substances or precursor chemicals to illicit markets or uses. This information may also be shared provincial regulatory authorities or law enforcement agencies, as outlined in the applicable regulations. Additionally, the information may be shared with other HC programs involved in compliance and enforcement, and may also be used internally to analyze trends, inform future actions, and evaluate the effectiveness of the program.

## Why a privacy impact assessment was completed

Personal information collected during the suspicious transaction reporting process may be used to support administrative decisions, such as conducting compliance verifications related to the party involved in the transaction.

As such, this Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) was completed in accordance with Section C.2.2.9.4 of the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat (TBS) Directive on Privacy Practices, which requires HC to conduct a PIA for existing programs or activities that use personal information for an administrative purpose and do not already have a Personal Information Bank (PIB).

## Additional information

This PIA focused on HC's Office of Controlled Substance’s (OCS) collection, use, disclosure and retention and disposition of personal information involved in the reporting of suspicious transaction for controlled substances and precursor chemicals. The Program is actively working to address these areas as part of its ongoing efforts to enhance privacy protections:

- **Information sharing agreements:** The Program will develop an information-sharing protocol or process that can be used for information sharing with law enforcement and with provincial regulators.
- **Access controls, safeguards and auditing:** The Program will consult with HC’s IT security to determine solutions to establish secure transmission protocols for Protected A information. In addition, the program will implement and document access review procedures for systems containing personal information to reduce the risk of unauthorized access and privacy breaches.
- **Transparency and individual awareness:** To enhance transparency and ensure compliance with the Treasury Board Secretariat Directive on Privacy Practices, the Program is updating its privacy notice statement to include all required elements. In addition, the Program will publish a PIB specific to this activity to meet Privacy Act requirements and support individuals’ rights to access their personal information.
