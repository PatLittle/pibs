# Information Sharing Authority for National Security and Financial Integrity

## Executive summary

### Introduction

In 2023, the Proceeds of *Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act* (PCMLTFA) was amended to provide a new information sharing authority (s. 53.32) to support the Minister of Finance’s (Minister) responsibilities in relation to national security and financial system integrity.

Under the authority, the Director of the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) may disclose to the Minister, upon request, information under FINTRAC’s control that relates to national security or to safeguarding the integrity of Canada’s financial system.

The Minister may only use FINTRAC information obtained under this authority in determining statutory approvals regarding federally regulated financial institutions, or in exercising authorities under the *Retail Payment Activities Act* (RPAA) to address national security risks that could be posed by retail payment service providers.

Given that personal information may be obtained by the Minister under this new authority, the Department of Finance Canada (Finance Canada) conducted a privacy impact assessment (PIA) to identify and mitigate any privacy risks.

### Findings

The PIA found that the proposed activities in connection with the use of the authority are compliant with the relevant sections of the *Privacy Act*. This includes sections related to collection authority, retention, accuracy, and use, among others.

The PIA also found that possible privacy risks are well-mitigated. Finance Canada will be using established information technology and management systems to transmit, store, and retain personal information in connection with this authority. These systems are compliant with relevant federal privacy and security policy requirements. Finance Canada will also limit access to the personal information to authorized staff on a limited basis.

### Recommendations

The PIA recommended that, given that the authority is new, additional actions should be undertaken by Finance Canada to promote greater certainty and awareness about how the authority should be administered in practice. This includes developing administrative procedures and standardized request templates as well as ensuring that authorized staff are aware of their roles and responsibilities. Finance Canada is actioning these recommendations.
