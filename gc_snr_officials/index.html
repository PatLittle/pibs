About this explorer

This proof of concept brings public appointment, designated-role and disclosure evidence together into candidate person records. It supports source review; it is not an official directory or a verified employment history.

Data integration pipeline

Sources and refresh behaviour

A GitHub Action collects the appointments index, follows organization profiles for appointment dates, and collects the seven designated-role lists. It commits each source as a CSV and publishes this directory independently. The Action runs daily at 08:00 UTC and can also be run manually. If a source fails or yields an unexpected empty result, its last good CSV is retained and its status is reported below. Reload committed data reloads these published files; it does not contact upstream sites. The same Action downloads the complete Open Government travel and hospitality JSON dumps and rebuilds counted combinations. It also reads processed-csvs/orders.csv at a pinned commit of PatLittle/oic-data to rebuild OIC candidate evidence. All output files remain local to this explorer at runtime; the status table shows each source’s last successful refresh.

Combinations and observations

Disclosure rows were grouped by exact name, English position title and organization identifier within each source. The latest dump row counts and retained combination counts are shown in Source coverage. Each combination retains its row count and earliest/latest valid disclosure dates. Individual disclosure rows are not loaded into the browser.

Number of observations adds those retained row counts and one for each appointment-index or designated-role record. It counts source observations, not unique events. The person dialog retains all combinations and their counts. Orders in Council searches provide supporting evidence on demand and do not increase this total. Records without a usable name cannot form a candidate.

Dates

For appointments, the first/last columns use original/current appointment dates from the organization profile. Expiry dates and access dates do not extend the appointment observation window. Access dates remain in the person dialog. Missing appointment dates stay Unknown. Other sources use disclosure dates, effective dates, or snapshot access dates when no event date exists. Across sources the columns show the earliest/latest of those evidence dates; they do not establish continuous tenure. Invalid or implausible disclosure dates are excluded and flagged in source details.

Name presentation and provenance

The retrieval scripts retain name_original and name_normalized on every source observation, with name as a compact field that removes commas, periods and all whitespace from the normalized form. The table displays this compact name; readable normalized and verbatim original names remain in the person dialog. Clear family-name-first entries are reordered to given name, middle names or initials, then family name. Spelling, accents, capitalization and initials are preserved. Recognized honorifics/ranks and generational suffixes are stored separately. Names without a comma retain their supplied order; ambiguous comma patterns remain unchanged and are flagged for review. These rules cannot infer missing names or resolve every cultural naming convention.

OIC evidence matching

The Action matches complete observed normalized names in order précis text. Matched original and normalized text, order dates and official attachment links are retained. A name mention is candidate evidence, not proof of appointment or identity; absent matches are not proof that no order exists. The OIC mirror’s source commit, latest order date and downloaded-file hash are recorded.

Possible same-person matches and identity likelihood

The Action uses carltonnorthern/nicknames (Apache 2.0 license) plus a separately labelled supplement for the user-provided Alexandre → Alex example. Direct nickname relations and two nicknames sharing the same canonical given name generate pairs within a family-name-token block. No transitive identity merging is performed. Alex/Alexandre Benay remain linked, reviewable candidates with their own source evidence. The lookup is incomplete and culturally uneven; absent links do not mean different people.

Each pair starts at 60 for a direct nickname relation or 55 for shared canonical names. The strongest source-combination pair adds 18 for matching institution labels, 17 for matching/similar role labels (token Jaccard similarity ≥ 0.65), and 5 for overlapping event windows with a shared institution or role. Non-overlapping windows add 10 when within five years, otherwise 5, reflecting a possible career transition. Overlapping windows at different institutions and roles are neutral because disclosure spans do not establish tenure. Compatible middle names or initials add 5; conflicting middle names subtract 30 and cap the result at 49. Missing evidence adds nothing; access dates are excluded. Scores are capped at 95, with 80+ labelled strong, 60–79 probable, and lower scores requiring review. These are explainable heuristics, not statistical probabilities or verified identities. The table shows the highest pair score; the person dialog explains each pair.

Matching and consistency

Names are lowercased, accents and punctuation normalized, and comma-separated family/given names reordered. Candidates share the first given-name and last family-name tokens. This can combine different people or separate variants of one person. All observed name, institution and role variants remain available for review.

The consistency score weights name-form consistency at 55%, the dominant position label at 25%, and the dominant institution label at 20%. It measures agreement among retained source combinations, not identity certainty, seniority, legal obligations or data accuracy. The table shows up to 1,000 candidates after filtering and sorting; summaries count all loaded candidates.
