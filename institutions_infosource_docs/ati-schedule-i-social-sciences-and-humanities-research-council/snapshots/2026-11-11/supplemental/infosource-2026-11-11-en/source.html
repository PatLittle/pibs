Info Source

Info Source describes the programs and activities—and the information holdings related to programs and activities—of government institutions subject to the Access to Information Act to facilitate the right of access. It also provides individuals, including current and former Government of Canada employees, with relevant information to access personal information about themselves held by government institutions subject to the Privacy Act and exercise their rights under the Privacy Act.

An index of institutions subject to the Access to Information Act and the Privacy Act are available centrally.

The Access to Information Act and the Privacy Act assign overall responsibility to the president of the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat (as the designated minister) for the government-wide administration of the legislation.

Institution-specific content

Insight Research: new knowledge in the social sciences and humanities

The Insight Research program provides grants to support research in the social sciences and humanities conducted by scholars and researchers working as individuals, in teams and in formal partnerships among the academic, public, private and/or not-for-profit sectors, to support the building of institutional research capacity. This program is necessary to build knowledge and understanding about people, societies and the world, and informs the search for solutions to societal challenges. The program aims to build knowledge and understanding from disciplinary, interdisciplinary and/or cross-sector perspectives; support new approaches to research on complex and important topics; provide a high-quality research training experience for students; mobilize research knowledge to and from academic and non-academic audiences; and build institutional research capacity. Research supported by the program has the potential to have intellectual, cultural, social and economic influence, benefit and impact, and increase institutional research capacity. International research initiatives that offer outstanding opportunities to advance Canadian research are encouraged. Partnerships can include both Canadian and international partners.

Insight Research grants

This program provides grants to support research in the social sciences and humanities conducted by scholars and researchers working as individuals and/or in teams, including international initiatives. The program leverages external funding through collaborative partnerships. The goal of SSHRC’s Insight Research program is to build knowledge and understanding about people, societies and the world by supporting research excellence in all subject areas eligible for SSHRC funding.

Insight Research funding opportunities class of record

Description: Includes records related to the administration of current and completed programs supporting investigator-chosen research conducted by individuals and small teams. Current opportunities include: the College and Community Innovation Program; Insight Grants; Insight Development Grants; Connection Grants; the Department of National Defence Research Initiative; and the Sport Participation Research Initiative. Concluded funding opportunities include: Aboriginal Research Grants; the Canadian Initiative on Social Statistics; Community-University Research Alliances; Major Collaborative Research Initiatives; Research-Creation in Fine Arts; and Standard Research Grants. Records include information on the description, development, planning, administration and ongoing evolution of the program.

Document types: Application forms and procedures; eligibility criteria; terms and conditions of funding opportunities; business processes; project proposals; training plans; reviewer and recipient templates; evaluation reports; final research reports or achievement reports (as applicable); reports on the use of grant funds; financial administration forms; briefing notes and memoranda; project charters; surveys (including self-identification questionnaire); and attestation documents.

Disclosure summaries: Under development.

Institutional Research Capacity Grants

This program provides grants to Canadian postsecondary institutions for the development of research capacity in the social sciences and humanities. This program is intended to increase research excellence and strengthen research capacity by covering expenses that include the start-up costs of research centres; support for visiting scholars (travel and stipend); help for emerging scholars to become competitive in national-level grants competition; and support for national and international dissemination and collaboration.

Institutional research capacity funding opportunities class of record

Records relate to the description, development, planning, administration and ongoing evolution of the program.

Document types: Application procedures and eligibility criteria; terms and conditions of funding opportunities; business processes; project plans; budgets; reports on the use of grant funds; briefing notes and memoranda; financial administration forms; and statistical and competition reports.

Record number: SSHRC RES 090

Grants and awards management personal information bank

Description: This bank describes the personal information of individuals who apply to SSHRC grants and awards and/or participate in the merit review of applications. This may include: name; contact information; year of birth; language of correspondence; citizenship; social insurance number; file number; financial information; biographical details; educational information; institutional affiliation; and the opinions and views of, or about, the individual. It also includes self-identification information (date of birth, gender identity, sexual orientation, Indigenous identity, visible minority population group, person with disability). Indigenous affirmation data related to the applicant’s Indigenous citizenship and membership may be collected. This bank may also include personal information from publicly available information such as name; contact information; organizational/institutional affiliation; disciplines of research; research expertise; career history; and publications.

Personal information includes external assessor and peer review committee evaluations; scores; recommendations; and decisions. It also includes information reported by award recipients on the outcomes of their SSHRC-funded research or research-related activities, including personal information about individuals that participated on the research team.

Note: This personal information bank is currently under review.

Class of individuals: Graduate students; scholars; former applicants and recipients of SSHRC funding; individuals who are selected to be assessors or reviewers for applications; employees from all levels of government, individuals from not-for-profit and private sectors; and members of academic and non-academic organizations.

Purpose: This information is collected to determine eligibility of applicants for funding opportunities; notify successful applicants of subsequent reporting requirements; constitute a history of SSHRC’s support to individuals and organizations; administer and monitor awards; identify and select prospective assessors/reviewers of applications; and establish and maintain a pool of potential merit review experts and committee members. For certain funding opportunities, self-identification information is collected and used for targeted funding related to the applicant’s self-identification data. Self-identification information is also collected for non-administrative purposes, including program evaluations and aggregate reporting. Indigenous affirmation data is collected to support a claim of Indigenous membership or citizenship for targeted funding eligibility. Information about the applicant and research team’s institutional affiliation and attestation is collected and used for funding applications that involve advancing a sensitive technology research area to protect against risks to national security under the Government of Canada’s Policy on Sensitive Technology Research and Affiliations of Concern and National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships. Publicly available information is collected for the identification and recruitment of prospective assessors/reviewers of applications; to validate information submitted by the applicant in their application; and for public relations purposes as applicable. Personal information from post-research achievement reports is collected for non-administrative purposes to monitor and measure program performance, for program planning, and for communication and promotional activities.

Personal information is collected pursuant to the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council Act section 4(1)(a). The social insurance number is collected pursuant to the Income Tax Act, section 237(1)(b) to issue tax slips.

Consistent uses: The personal information may be used and disclosed by SSHRC for the following purposes:

  • To consult with other federal research funding agencies to determine the most appropriate review mechanism or source of funding, or to monitor overlap in funding support. In some cases, where applications are deemed to be more appropriately supported by another federal agency, the complete applications may be transferred to facilitate and expedite the merit review process.
  • With consent, personal information may be shared with other agencies and departments of the Government of Canada for the purpose of joint program administration and delivery (see complete list of joint initiatives), as well as with other organizations that have established agreements with SSHRC and are specifically interested in supporting the research and research-related activities.
  • SSHRC routinely publishes and disseminates certain details about successful applications. This can include the name of the applicant(s); award type; amount awarded; institution and department; discipline or field of research; summary of the research or research-related activity submitted by the applicant; and project title. The summary submitted by the applicant of the research or research-related activities can be disclosed for public reference upon request.
  • With consent, self-identification information may be shared with administering institutions and funding partners for the purposes of program delivery and reporting in aggregate.
  • Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the Tri-Agency Framework: Responsible Conduct of Research.
  • Indigenous affirmation data may be used and disclosed for compliance and enforcement per the Tri-agency Policy on Indigenous Citizenship and Membership Affirmation.
  • Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the Policy on Sensitive Technology Research and Affiliations of Concern and National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships.
  • Personal information may also be used and disclosed by SSHRC for program planning, design and delivery; data and statistical analysis, research, performance measurement; and monitoring, audit, evaluation and review, including aggregate reporting to senior management, Parliament and the ministers of Industry and Health.
  • SSHRC uses the information in its files and databases to generate mailing lists, for public relations purposes, for stakeholder engagement, to disseminate information to the research community, and to promote SSHRC and its funding opportunities.

Retention and disposal standards: Generally, data are retained for 30 years, with the exception of Indigenous affirmation data, which is retained for seven (7) years from the last administrative action and then disposed of in accordance with standards set by Library and Archives Canada.

Canada Research Chairs

This program provides support to research chairs in the form of salary and research funding to attract and retain talent in the social sciences and humanities, natural sciences and engineering, and health. National and international researchers can be chairholders. This program is necessary to strengthen Canadian research excellence; improve research training of highly qualified personnel; improve universities’ capacity to generate and apply new knowledge; and promote the best possible use of research resources through strategic institutional planning and collaboration among institutions and between sectors. The Canada Research Chairs Program is a tri-agency initiative of SSHRC, the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council (NSERC) and the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR), and is administered by the Tri-agency Institutional Programs Secretariat, which is housed within SSHRC.

Personal information about the nominees/applicants, chairs and reviewers may include: full name; contact information; year or date of birth; curriculum vitae; funding currently or previously held; occupation; professional affiliation and experience; research proposals; letters of reference; citizenship (Canadian, permanent resident or other); language of correspondence (English or French); career interruptions; funding requested; and the amount awarded. This bank may include personal information from publicly available information such as name; contact information; organizational/institutional affiliation; disciplines of research; research expertise; career history; and publications.

Note: This personal information bank reflects the privacy practices associated with the following research funding opportunities: Canada Research Chairs Program; Canada Excellence Research Chairs Program; Canada First Research Excellence Fund; Canada 150 Research Chairs Program; and the Canada Biomedical Research Fund.

Class of individuals: Academic and independent scholars, research teams associated with the award, representatives from postsecondary institutions, and individuals from the private sector and academic and non-academic organizations who provide independent assessments.

Purpose: The personal information is collected for chair nomination/application purposes; merit review, which includes identifying and selecting prospective external assessors and adjudication committee members, as well as establishing and maintaining a pool of potential reviewers; award/chair selection; post-award monitoring; and program administration. Self-identification information is collected and used for the purpose of targeted funding related to the applicant’s self-identification data. Self-identification information is also collected for non-administrative purposes, to ensure fulfilment of performance measure strategy objectives through aggregate reporting and program evaluations; to determine whether there are systemic barriers that may affect individuals from designated groups; and to assess postsecondary institutional commitment to ensuring opportunities are made available to the four designated groups. Indigenous affirmation data is collected to support a claim of Indigenous membership or citizenship for targeted funding eligibility. Publicly available information is collected for the identification and recruitment of prospective assessors/reviewers of applications, to validate information submitted by the applicant in their application, and for promotional purposes as applicable.

Personal information is collected under the authority of section 4(1)(a) of the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council Act, section 4(1)(a) of the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council Act, and section 5(a) of the Canadian Institutions of Health Research Act.

Consistent uses: The personal information may be used and disclosed for the following purposes:

  • Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the Tri-Agency Framework: Responsible Conduct of Research.
  • Indigenous affirmation data may be used and disclosed for compliance and enforcement per the Tri-agency Policy on Indigenous Citizenship and Membership Affirmation.
  • Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the Policy on Sensitive Technology Research and Affiliations of Concern and National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships.
  • Personal information may also be used for program planning, design and delivery; data and statistical analysis; research and performance measurement; and monitoring, audit, evaluation and review, including aggregate reporting to senior management, Parliament and the ministers of Industry and Health.
  • SSHRC uses the information in its files and databases to generate mailing lists, for public relations purposes, for stakeholder engagement, to disseminate information to the research community and to promote SSHRC and its funding opportunities.

Retention and disposal standards: Generally, data are retained for 30 years, with the exception of Indigenous affirmation data which is retained for seven (7) years from the last administrative action and then disposed of in accordance with standards set by Library and Archives Canada.

Research Training and Talent Development

The Research Training and Talent Development program provides support to graduate students and postdoctoral fellows in the form of fellowships, and to research chairs in postsecondary institutions to cover salary and research funding. This program is necessary to attract, retain and develop talent in the social sciences and humanities; cultivate leaders within academia and across the public, private and not-for-profit sectors; and build centres of world-class research excellence at Canadian postsecondary institutions. The program brands Canada as a top destination for research and research training.

Canada Graduate Research Scholarships

This program provides scholarships to Canadian master’s and doctoral students in the social sciences and humanities who demonstrate a high standard of achievement in their undergraduate and graduate studies. The program is necessary for graduate students to develop the research skills needed to become highly qualified personnel and research leaders of the future.

Research Partnerships

The Research Partnerships program provides funding (grants and operational) to support the multidirectional flow, exchange and co-creation of knowledge in the social sciences and humanities among researchers and diverse groups of policy-makers, business leaders, community groups, educators and the media working as individuals, in teams, in formal partnerships and in networks. This program is necessary to help stimulate cutting-edge, internationally competitive research in areas critical to Canada; build multisectoral partnerships; and accelerate the use of multidisciplinary research results by organizations that can harness them for Canadian economic and social development. The program increases the availability and use of social sciences and humanities research knowledge among academic and non-academic audiences; supports the building of reciprocal relationships, networks and tools designed to facilitate scholarly work; and makes such networks and tools more accessible to non-academic audiences. The funding opportunities offered in this program are intended to complement activities funded through the Research Training and Talent Development and Insight programs.

Research Partnerships and Knowledge Mobilization grants

This program provides grants to support the knowledge mobilization activities of scholars and researchers working as individuals, in teams, and in formal partnerships with the academic, public, private and/or not-for-profit sectors. This program is necessary to build links between the social sciences and humanities research community and potential users of research to maximize the impact of research beyond academia and allow for fruitful exchanges and the multidirectional flow of knowledge, foster an entrepreneurial spirit. Funding opportunities support the dissemination of research results through publications of various types, such as scholarly journals and books; the organization of events for researchers to meet, discuss, compare and plan research activities; and the co-creation, synthesis and application of research knowledge. The program leverages external funding through collaborative knowledge partnerships and joint initiatives with national or international agencies, government departments and organizations across the public, private and not-for-profit sectors.

Canada Biomedical Research Fund

The Canada Biomedical Research Fund (CBRF) supports high-risk, applied research, training and talent development partnership projects as part of a suite of investments included in Canada’s Biomanufacturing and Life Sciences Strategy to establish a resilient and robust domestic biomanufacturing sector in Canada. Research chairs nominations and peer review committee personal information bank

Description: This bank describes personal information that is used in support of nominations/applications submitted by postsecondary institutions, as well as information related to the recruitment and adjudication processes and administration, evaluation and monitoring of awards for the support and development of research and for research tools and equipment. Personal information about the nominees/applicants, chairs and reviewers may include: full name; contact information; year or date of birth; curriculum vitae; funding currently or previously held; occupation; professional affiliation and experience; research proposals; letters of reference; citizenship (Canadian, permanent resident or other); language of correspondence (English or French); career interruptions; funding requested; and the amount awarded. It also includes self-identification information (date of birth, gender identity, sexual orientation, Indigenous identity, visible minority population group, person with disability). Personal information includes external assessor and peer review committee evaluations; scores; recommendations; and decisions. This bank also contains the financial documentation generated in the administration of these awards. Full name, contact information and preferred language of correspondence are collected from the representatives of the postsecondary institutions for communications and application purposes. This bank may include personal information from publicly available information such as name; contact information; organizational/institutional affiliation; disciplines of research; research expertise; career history; and publications.

Note: This personal information bank reflects the privacy practices associated with the following research funding opportunities: Canada Research Chairs Program; Canada Excellence Research Chairs Program; Canada First Research Excellence Fund; Canada 150 Research Chairs Program; and Canada Biomedical Research Fund.

Class of individuals: Academic and independent scholars, research teams associated with the award, representatives from postsecondary institutions, and individuals from the private sector and academic and non-academic organizations who provide independent assessments.

Purpose: The personal information is collected for chair nomination/application purposes; merit review, which includes identifying and selecting prospective external assessors and adjudication committee members as well as establishing and maintaining a pool of potential reviewers; award/chair selection; post-award monitoring; and program administration. Self-identification information is collected and used for the purpose of targeted funding related to the applicant’s self-identification data. Self-identification information is also collected for non-administrative purposes, to ensure fulfilment of performance measure strategy objectives through aggregate reporting and program evaluations; to determine whether there are systemic barriers that may affect individuals from designated groups; and to assess postsecondary institutional commitment to ensuring opportunities are made available to the four designated groups. Indigenous affirmation data is collected to support a claim of Indigenous membership or citizenship for targeted funding eligibility. Publicly available information is collected for the identification and recruitment of prospective assessors/reviewers of applications, to validate information submitted by the applicant in their application, and for promotional purposes as applicable.

Personal information is collected under the authority of section 4(1)(a) of the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council Act, section 4(1)(a) of the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council Act, and section 5(a) of the Canadian Institutions of Health Research Act.

Consistent uses: The personal information may be used and disclosed for the following purposes:

  • In consultation with partner agencies and institutions as required. It can be disclosed to partner agencies for the purposes of reviewing applications, administering awards and monitoring and evaluating the program.
  • Information may be exchanged with Innovation, Science and Economic Development (ISED) Canada and the Canada Foundation for Innovation (CFI) for the purposes of considering joint funding requests for additional infrastructure support.
  • With consent, self-identification information may be shared with administering institutions and funding partners for the purposes of program delivery and reporting in aggregate.
  • Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the Tri-Agency Framework: Responsible Conduct of Research.
  • Indigenous affirmation data may be used and disclosed for compliance and enforcement per the Tri-agency Policy on Indigenous Citizenship and Membership Affirmation.
  • Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the Policy on Sensitive Technology Research and Affiliations of Concern and National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships.
  • Personal information may also be used for program planning, design and delivery; data and statistical analysis; research and performance measurement; and monitoring, audit, evaluation and review, including aggregate reporting to senior management, Parliament and the ministers of Industry and Health.
  • SSHRC uses the information in its files and databases to generate mailing lists, for public relations purposes, for stakeholder engagement, to disseminate information to the research community, and to promote SSHRC and its funding opportunities.

Retention and disposal standards: Generally, data are retained for 30 years, with the exception of Indigenous affirmation data, which is retained for seven (7) years from the last administrative action and then disposed of in accordance with standards set by Library and Archives Canada.

Internal services are groups of related activities and resources administered to support the needs of programs and other corporate obligations of an organization. These groups are: Acquisitions; Communications services; Financial management; Human resources management; Information management; Information technology; Legal services; Management and oversight services; Material; Real property; and Travel and other administrative services. Classes of personal information

Administration files

SSHRC’s general administrative and correspondence files contain a certain amount of personal information not arranged or retrievable by personal identifiers. Such personal information includes suggestions and opinions from the general public, which form part of a specific subject file. This form of personal information is normally retrievable only if specifics of name, date of communication and subject are provided. The retention periods for this class of personal information are controlled by the retention schedules of the files in which they are stored.

Reports and surveys

For purposes of program planning and evaluation, SSHRC requires a variety of reports concerning aspects of social sciences and humanities research in Canada. The reports themselves, published or unpublished, do not contain personal information about individuals. In the process of preparing such reports, personal information is necessarily obtained. This information may include current academic position; professional qualifications of individuals; nature of research being undertaken by individuals; and present and past funding of an individual’s research. This is not arranged or retrievable by personal identifiers. This form of personal information is normally retrievable only if specifics are provided concerning the study or report involved. The retention periods for this class of personal information are controlled by the retention schedules of the files in which they are stored.
