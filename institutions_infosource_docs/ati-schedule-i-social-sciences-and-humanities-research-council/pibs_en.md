# Info Source

Info Source describes the programs and activities—and the information holdings related to programs and activities—of government institutions subject to the [*Access to Information Act*](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-1/) to facilitate the right of access. It also provides individuals, including current and former Government of Canada employees, with relevant information to access personal information about themselves held by government institutions subject to the [*Privacy Act*](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/ENG/ACTS/P-21/index.html) and exercise their rights under the *Privacy Act*.

An [index of institutions](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/access-information-privacy/access-information/info-source/list-institutions.html) subject to the *Access to Information Act* and the *Privacy Act* are available centrally.

The *Access to Information Act* and the *Privacy Act* assign overall responsibility to the president of the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat (as the designated minister) for the government-wide administration of the legislation.

### Institution-specific content

#### Insight Research: new knowledge in the social sciences and humanities

The Insight Research program provides grants to support research in the social sciences and humanities conducted by scholars and researchers working as individuals, in teams and in formal partnerships among the academic, public, private and/or not-for-profit sectors, to support the building of institutional research capacity. This program is necessary to build knowledge and understanding about people, societies and the world, and informs the search for solutions to societal challenges. The program aims to build knowledge and understanding from disciplinary, interdisciplinary and/or cross-sector perspectives; support new approaches to research on complex and important topics; provide a high-quality research training experience for students; mobilize research knowledge to and from academic and non-academic audiences; and build institutional research capacity. Research supported by the program has the potential to have intellectual, cultural, social and economic influence, benefit and impact, and increase institutional research capacity. International research initiatives that offer outstanding opportunities to advance Canadian research are encouraged. Partnerships can include both Canadian and international partners.

##### Insight Research grants

This program provides grants to support research in the social sciences and humanities conducted by scholars and researchers working as individuals and/or in teams, including international initiatives. The program leverages external funding through collaborative partnerships. The goal of SSHRC’s Insight Research program is to build knowledge and understanding about people, societies and the world by supporting research excellence in all subject areas eligible for SSHRC funding.

##### Institutional Research Capacity Grants

This program provides grants to Canadian postsecondary institutions for the development of research capacity in the social sciences and humanities. This program is intended to increase research excellence and strengthen research capacity by covering expenses that include the start-up costs of research centres; support for visiting scholars (travel and stipend); help for emerging scholars to become competitive in national-level grants competition; and support for national and international dissemination and collaboration.

Grants and awards management personal information bank

**Description:** This bank describes the personal information of individuals who apply to SSHRC grants and awards and/or participate in the merit review of applications. This may include: name; contact information; year of birth; language of correspondence; citizenship; social insurance number; file number; financial information; biographical details; educational information; institutional affiliation; and the opinions and views of, or about, the individual. It also includes self-identification information (date of birth, gender identity, sexual orientation, Indigenous identity, visible minority population group, person with disability). Indigenous affirmation data related to the applicant’s Indigenous citizenship and membership may be collected. This bank may also include personal information from publicly available information such as name; contact information; organizational/institutional affiliation; disciplines of research; research expertise; career history; and publications.

Personal information includes external assessor and peer review committee evaluations; scores; recommendations; and decisions. It also includes information reported by award recipients on the outcomes of their SSHRC-funded research or research-related activities, including personal information about individuals that participated on the research team.

**Note:** This personal information bank is currently under review.

**Class of individuals:** Graduate students; scholars; former applicants and recipients of SSHRC funding; individuals who are selected to be assessors or reviewers for applications; employees from all levels of government, individuals from not-for-profit and private sectors; and members of academic and non-academic organizations.

**Purpose:** This information is collected to determine eligibility of applicants for funding opportunities; notify successful applicants of subsequent reporting requirements; constitute a history of SSHRC’s support to individuals and organizations; administer and monitor awards; identify and select prospective assessors/reviewers of applications; and establish and maintain a pool of potential merit review experts and committee members. For certain funding opportunities, self-identification information is collected and used for targeted funding related to the applicant’s self-identification data. Self-identification information is also collected for non-administrative purposes, including program evaluations and aggregate reporting. Indigenous affirmation data is collected to support a claim of Indigenous membership or citizenship for targeted funding eligibility. Information about the applicant and research team’s institutional affiliation and attestation is collected and used for funding applications that involve advancing a sensitive technology research area to protect against risks to national security under the Government of Canada’s [Policy on Sensitive Technology Research and Affiliations of Concern](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/safeguarding-your-research/guidelines-and-tools-implement-research-security/sensitive-technology-research-and-affiliations-concern/policy-sensitive-technology-research-and-affiliations-concern) and [National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/safeguarding-your-research/guidelines-and-tools-implement-research-security/national-security-guidelines-research-partnerships). Publicly available information is collected for the identification and recruitment of prospective assessors/reviewers of applications; to validate information submitted by the applicant in their application; and for public relations purposes as applicable. Personal information from post-research achievement reports is collected for non-administrative purposes to monitor and measure program performance, for program planning, and for communication and promotional activities.

Personal information is collected pursuant to the *Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council Act* section 4(1)(a). The social insurance number is collected pursuant to the *Income Tax Act*, section 237(1)(b) to issue tax slips.

**Consistent uses:** The personal information may be used and disclosed by SSHRC for the following purposes:

* To consult with other federal research funding agencies to determine the most appropriate review mechanism or source of funding, or to monitor overlap in funding support. In some cases, where applications are deemed to be more appropriately supported by another federal agency, the complete applications may be transferred to facilitate and expedite the merit review process.
* With consent, personal information may be shared with other agencies and departments of the Government of Canada for the purpose of joint program administration and delivery (see complete [list of joint initiatives](/en/funding/opportunities/joint-initiatives.aspx)), as well as with other organizations that have established agreements with SSHRC and are specifically interested in supporting the research and research-related activities.
* SSHRC routinely publishes and disseminates certain details about successful applications. This can include the name of the applicant(s); award type; amount awarded; institution and department; discipline or field of research; summary of the research or research-related activity submitted by the applicant; and project title. The summary submitted by the applicant of the research or research-related activities can be disclosed for public reference upon request.
* With consent, self-identification information may be shared with administering institutions and funding partners for the purposes of program delivery and reporting in aggregate.
* Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the [Tri-Agency Framework: Responsible Conduct of Research](https://www.rcr.ethics.gc.ca/eng/framework-cadre-2021.html).
* Indigenous affirmation data may be used and disclosed for compliance and enforcement per the [Tri-agency Policy on Indigenous Citizenship and Membership Affirmation](https://www.canada.ca/en/research-coordinating-committee/programs/policies-directives/tri-agency-policy-indigenous-citizenship-membership-affirmation.html).
* Information is used and disclosed for purposes consistent with the [Policy on Sensitive Technology Research and Affiliations of Concern](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/safeguarding-your-research/guidelines-and-tools-implement-research-security/sensitive-technology-research-and-affiliations-concern/policy-sensitive-technology-research-and-affiliations-concern) and [National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships.](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/safeguarding-your-research/guidelines-and-tools-implement-research-security/national-security-guidelines-research-partnerships)
* Personal information may also be used and disclosed by SSHRC for program planning, design and delivery; data and statistical analysis, research, performance measurement; and monitoring, audit, evaluation and review, including aggregate reporting to senior management, Parliament and the ministers of Industry and Health.
* SSHRC uses the information in its files and databases to generate mailing lists, for public relations purposes, for stakeholder engagement, to disseminate information to the research community, and to promote SSHRC and its funding opportunities.

**Retention and disposal standards:** Generally, data are retained for 30 years, with the exception of Indigenous affirmation data, which is retained for seven (7) years from the last administrative action and then disposed of in accordance with standards set by Library and Archives Canada.

##### Canada Research Chairs

This program provides support to research chairs in the form of salary and research funding to attract and retain talent in the social sciences and humanities, natural sciences and engineering, and health. National and international researchers can be chairholders. This program is necessary to strengthen Canadian research excellence; improve research training of highly qualified personnel; improve universities’ capacity to generate and apply new knowledge; and promote the best possible use of research resources through strategic institutional planning and collaboration among institutions and between sectors. The Canada Research Chairs Program is a tri-agency initiative of SSHRC, the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council (NSERC) and the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR), and is administered by the Tri-agency Institutional Programs Secretariat, which is housed within SSHRC.

#### Research Partnerships

The Research Partnerships program provides funding (grants and operational) to support the multidirectional flow, exchange and co-creation of knowledge in the social sciences and humanities among researchers and diverse groups of policy-makers, business leaders, community groups, educators and the media working as individuals, in teams, in formal partnerships and in networks. This program is necessary to help stimulate cutting-edge, internationally competitive research in areas critical to Canada; build multisectoral partnerships; and accelerate the use of multidisciplinary research results by organizations that can harness them for Canadian economic and social development. The program increases the availability and use of social sciences and humanities research knowledge among academic and non-academic audiences; supports the building of reciprocal relationships, networks and tools designed to facilitate scholarly work; and makes such networks and tools more accessible to non-academic audiences. The funding opportunities offered in this program are intended to complement activities funded through the Research Training and Talent Development and Insight programs.

## Classes of personal information

### Administration files

SSHRC’s general administrative and correspondence files contain a certain amount of personal information not arranged or retrievable by personal identifiers. Such personal information includes suggestions and opinions from the general public, which form part of a specific subject file. This form of personal information is normally retrievable only if specifics of name, date of communication and subject are provided. The retention periods for this class of personal information are controlled by the retention schedules of the files in which they are stored.

### Reports and surveys

For purposes of program planning and evaluation, SSHRC requires a variety of reports concerning aspects of social sciences and humanities research in Canada. The reports themselves, published or unpublished, do not contain personal information about individuals. In the process of preparing such reports, personal information is necessarily obtained. This information may include current academic position; professional qualifications of individuals; nature of research being undertaken by individuals; and present and past funding of an individual’s research. This is not arranged or retrievable by personal identifiers. This form of personal information is normally retrievable only if specifics are provided concerning the study or report involved. The retention periods for this class of personal information are controlled by the retention schedules of the files in which they are stored.

For general information about making a request for access to information or personal information, see [Make an access to information or personal information request](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/access-information-privacy/access-information/request-information.html).

To make a request for information online, see the [Access to information and personal information online request service](https://atip-aiprp.tbs-sct.gc.ca/welcome).

To make a request for information under the *Access to Information Act* or the *Privacy Act* by mail, send your letter or completed [Access to information request form](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/corporate/forms/350-57.html) (*Access to Information Act*) or [Personal information request form](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/corporate/forms/350-58.html) (*Privacy Act*), along with any necessary documents (such as consent or the $5 application fee for a request under the *Access to Information Act*), to the following address:

### Mail

ATIP Coordinator
Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council
125 Zaida Eddy Private, 2nd Floor
Ottawa, Ontario, Canada
K1R 0E3

### Email

ATIP-AIPRP@sshrc-crsh.gc.ca

In accordance with the *Access to Information Act* and the *Privacy Act*, an area on SSHRC premises will be made available to review original materials if that is the applicant’s preference (and it is practical to do so), or if it is not practical to create copies of the material.

**Note:**Each request made to SSHRC under the *Access to Information Act* must be accompanied by an application fee of $5. For requests made online, this is paid by credit card at the time of application. For requests made by mail, this fee should be paid by enclosing a cheque or money order made payable to the Receiver General for Canada.

The Government of Canada encourages the release of information through requests outside the formal request processes. To make an informal request, contact [SSHRC’s ATIP Coordinator](/en/about-sshrc/transparency/access-to-information-and-privacy.aspx).

You may also wish to search [summaries of completed access to information requests](https://open.canada.ca/en/search/ati?f%5B0%5D=ss_ati_organization_en%3ASocial%20Sciences%20and%20Humanities%20Research%20Council%20of%20Canada) for which SSHRC has already provided responses, as this information may be more easily obtained. You may also wish to review available open data regarding SSHRC.

SSHRC conducts privacy impact assessments (PIAs) to ensure that privacy implications will be appropriately identified, assessed and resolved before a new or substantially modified program or activity involving personal information is implemented. [Summaries of completed PIAs](/en/about-sshrc/transparency/privacy-impact-assessments.aspx) are available.
