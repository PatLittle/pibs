## National Child Exploitation Crime Centre Program

**Description:** This bank describes personal information collected in support of criminal investigations involving internet-facilitated sexual exploitation of children including information of victims and individuals involved in suspected criminal activities in the sexual exploitation of children on the Internet. Personal information is obtained from various sources including domestic and international anonymous reporting portals, Internet and Communication Service Providers, domestic and international law enforcement agencies, domestic and international government departments, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and through the use of operational technologies online.

Personal information collected may include child sexual abuse material (CSAM) consisting of videos, images, online communications or chats, online text-based materials and associated metadata such as cryptographic hash value(s), camera data embedded in an image such as the make/model of the camera, serial number, geo-coordinates such as latitude/longitude, file size, file name, user or camera owner name if available, suspected offender information such as name, contact information, type of criminal offence (child luring, child trafficking, possessing CSAM), IP addresses, port numbers, username/screen name of both victim and offender, IP logs and banking information including account holder name, city and province of the financial institution, and details related to the suspicious nature of transactions. Lawfully obtained images may be used by the NCECC in a face matching closed automated system to quickly sort, group and categorize large amounts of similar data to expedite human review and evaluation.

**Class of Individuals:** Children who are victims of online sexual exploitation and individuals implicated in, connected with or who are the subject of criminal investigations of online child sexual exploitation offences.

**Purpose:** Personal information is collected under the authority of section 18 of the RCMP Act and subsection 14(1) of the RCMP Regulations for the administration or enforcement of the law and in the detection, prevention or suppression of crime and under the Criminal Code of Canada for the investigation of child sexual exploitation.

**Consistent Uses:** Personal information described in this bank may be disclosed and used by domestic and foreign accredited law enforcement and investigative agencies for the administration, detection, prevention or suppression of online child sexual exploitation and of other crimes generally. This information may also be used for audit, research, evaluation and audit and statistical purposes and may be matched with information from other personal information banks and/or program records.

**Retention and Disposal Standards:** Personal information described in this bank is retained by the RCMP for 100 years with the exception of Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) (including associated metadata of such as cryptographic hash value, camera data embedded in an image such as the make/model of the camera, serial number, latitude/longitude and owner names where available) which is stored indefinitely. All personal information contained in this bank is retained on premise locally and is strictly managed by and under the control of the RCMP National Child Exploitation Crime Centre Program.

**RDA Number:** 2016/013

**Related Record Number:** RCMP OPS 1218, RCMP OPS 2123, RCMP OPS 1131

**TBS Registration:** 20250011

**Bank Number:** RCMP PPU 201

## RCMP Member Performance Review and Appraisal Records

**Description:** Information in this bank provides an overview of the member's work performance. The file contains such material as personal interview reports and appraisal reports, guidance and advice, commendations, awards, notification of shortcomings and related documents, copies of training applications, examination results and records of payment when identified in PERR. Also contained in this bank is compulsory discharge/demotion material in the case where a member of the RCMP is discharged and or demoted for unsatisfactory job performance. Training assessments may also be maintained in bank RCMP PPU 080. In addition to the requirements indicated on the Personal Information Request Form, individuals must provide their full name and regimental number. Serving members must indicate the location of the material they wish to access. Individuals wishing to access only specific information should identify the material desired to expedite the processing of their requests.

**Class of Individuals:** This bank contains personal information on individuals who are currently or who have served in the RCMP as regular, civilian, reserve or special constable members.

**Purpose:** The bank is used to make decisions on training, transfer, promotion, demotion, employee assistance, postings and termination of employee.

**Consistent Uses:** This information may also be used for research, planning, evaluation and audit and statistical analysis and may be matched with the following information banks: RCMP PPU 070 (Applicants and Cadets Records); RCMP PPU 090 (Honours and Awards); RCMP PPE 802 (RCMP Member Service Records); RCMP PPE 803 (RCMP Member Promotion Board Proceedings Records); RCMP PPE 804 (RCMP Member Grievance Records); RCMP PPE 805 (RCMP Member Discipline Records); RCMP PPU 085 (Complaints Against the RCMP or a Member, Enquiries and General Assistance); RCMP PPE 806 (RCMP Member's Pay and Allowance Records); RCMP PPE 815 (RCMP Member Conflict of Interest and Post Employment Code Records) and RCMP PPE 818 (Employment Equity Program). All linkages for the purpose of administering human resources and compensation plans are in compliance with the provisions of the Privacy Act. This information may be linked with the Human Resources Management Information System (HRMIS).

**Retention and Disposal Standards:** Information in this bank is retained at least until the subject of the file reaches the age of 100 years. If the file has been designated as having enduring value it will be transferred to the control of Library and Archives Canada, otherwise it will be retained by the RCMP until it no longer has business value. At that point, it will be destroyed.

**RDA Number:** 2000/030

**Related Record Number:** RCMP EMP 001

**TBS Registration:** 001013

**Bank Number:** RCMP PPE 801

## RCMP Member Service Records

**Description:** Information in this bank provides a chronological overview of the member's service. The file contains such things as application and engagement documents, basic cadet or recruit training results (LAN computer system), training assessments, recommendations for promotion and transfer, transfer and change notices, personal certificates, benefit related documents, statements of injuries and disabilities by members and witnesses, pensions, return to work and accommodation documentation, discharge documents including medical/compulsory discharge documents, form 1733 Discharge Request, demotion documents, warrants of appointment, outstanding debts, compassionate transfers, language results, linguistic profiles, Certificate of Security Clearance as well as the social insurance number, obtained under the authority of federal legislation to administer the Canada Pension Plan, the Employment Insurance Act, and the Income Tax Act. In addition to the requirements indicated on the Personal Information Request Form, individuals must provide their full name and regimental number. Serving members must indicate the location of the material they wish to access. Individuals wishing to access only specific information should identify the material desired to expedite the processing of their requests. As well, supplementary files are also retained at Headquarters and divisions on individuals who have successfully completed an undercover course and have been given by Headquarters an Operator's number. If you wish to access these records, advise as to whether you were trained as an undercover operator and/or given an operator number. Also indicate in which Division/Unit the records are retained. Information in this bank may be maintained in hard copy files as well as in automated forms such as the RCMP Office Support System (ROSS).

**Class of Individuals:** This bank contains personal information on individuals who are currently or who have served in the RCMP as regular, civilian, reserve or special constable members.

**Purpose:** This information, including social insurance numbers, is used for the internal administration of the RCMP and its pension and benefit program. To assess eligibility to contribute to various plans, to calculate retirement or survivor pensions, to maintain employment records, for employee benefit statements, to respond to enquiries from members from other plans and executors of wills, and to determine dental plan eligibility. This data is shared with Health Canada for the administration of the Canada Pension Plan. Information from this bank is provided to the contracted company for the purpose of pension and insurance administration. This information is afforded the necessary protection as required by the RCMP and is included within the terms of the contract.

**Consistent Uses:** This information may be used for research, planning, evaluation and audit and statistical analysis and verifications of pension coverage. It may be matched with the following banks :RCMP PPU 070 (Applicants and Cadets Records); RCMP PPU 090 (Honours and Awards); RCMP PPE 801 (RCMP Member Performance Review and Appraisal Records); RCMP PPE 803 (RCMP Member Promotion Board Proceedings Records); RCMP PPE 804 (RCMP Member Grievance Records); RCMP PPE 805 (RCMP Member Discipline); RCMP PPU 085 (Complaints Against the RCMP or a Member, Enquiries and General Assistance); RCMP PPE 806 (RCMP Member's Pay and Allowance Records); DND PPE 859 (Canadian Forces Pension Data Bank); PWGSC PCU 702 (Federal Pension Administration); NHW PCU 155 (Canada Pension Plan Computer Master Data bank and Quebec Pension Plan); RCMP PPE 815 (RCMP Member Conflict of Interest and Post Employment Code Records) and RCMP PPE 818 (Employment Equity Program). All linkages for the purpose of administering human resources and compensation plans are in compliance with the provisions of the Privacy Act. Information is supplied to Public Works Government Services Canada to provide and account for pension funds and specified insurance claims, records of which are maintained in - Personal Information Banks under. Public Works Government Services Canada. It may also be used to support applications for disability benefits in respect of injury or death on service under the RCMP Superannuation Act, section32, or under the RCMP Pension Continuation Act, section 5. Assessments and evaluations concerning pensions for disability or death are maintained in - Personal Information Banks under the Department of Veterans Affairs and must be accessed through that department. This information may be linked with the Human Resources Management Information System (HRMIS).

**Retention and Disposal Standards:** Information in this bank is retained at least until the subject of the file reaches the age of 100 years. If the file has been designated as having enduring value it will be transferred to the control of Library and Archives Canada, otherwise it will be retained by the RCMP until it no longer has business value. At that point, it will be destroyed.

**RDA Number:** 2000/030

## RCMP Member Conduct

**Description:** This bank describes personal information collected in support of the application of conduct standards for the RCMP and the administration of the conduct process established pursuant to Part IV of the RCMP Act, including the outcome of conduct processes, decisions on the merit of the allegation(s), conduct measures imposed pursuant to sections 3, 4 and 5 of the Commissioner Standing Orders (Conduct), related appeals, referrals to and findings and recommendations of the External Review Committee, and applications for judicial review.

In addition, this bank describes personal information related to conduct proceedings under Part IV, including information or complaints received and used to determine if an investigation should be mandated or other appropriate action taken, investigation reports, prima facie findings made by conduct authorities, interim measures (e.g. temporary reassignment, suspension, stoppage of pay and allowances), conduct meetings, conduct hearings, appeals and records of decision, documents related to statutory offences by members, court decisions and appeals including investigations dealing with complaints from the public resulting in conduct measures against a member and compulsory discharge material resulting from misconduct or criminal offences. Compulsory discharge material for reasons other than misconduct and criminal offences may be located in Bank RCMP PPE 801, RCMP Member Performance Review and Appraisal Records or RCMP PPE 802 RCMP Member Service Records.

Personal information may include, name, contact information, biographical information, date of birth, member identification number, member personnel information, financial information, medical information, legal advice, nature of the misconduct and measures, opinions, views of or about individuals and signature.

**Class of Individuals:** Regular and civilian members, as well as special constable members, who are currently serving or who have served in the RCMP and are/were the subject of alleged misconduct or conduct proceedings. This bank also contains information of interviewees and witnesses, which may include public complainants, affected employees and other affected persons.

**Purpose:** This bank is used to make decisions concerning alleged misconduct which can result in interim measures, findings as to whether the alleged misconduct is established, conduct measures imposed by conduct authorities or conduct boards, discharges, civil actions, criminal prosecutions, and investigations.

**Consistent Uses:** The RCMP External Review Committee and the Civilian Review and Complaints Commission for the RCMP may use the information respectively to inquire into grievances, appeals or investigate complaints against the RCMP or its members. Where applicable, information may be shared with the following entities: 1) RCMP National Human Rights Policy Centre for complaint management; 2) RCMP Departmental Security Branch for investigation purposes; 3) RCMP National Employment Requirements Policy Centre for employment requirement purposes; and 4) law enforcement agencies in the event of an alleged criminal offence. Crown prosecutors may disclose the information in criminal matters pursuant to the decision of the Supreme Court of Canada in R.V. McNeil 2009 SCC 3, [2009] S.C.R. 66, including when an allegation of misconduct has been established on a balance of probabilities or when the conduct authority has made a prima facie finding that the subject member’s conduct amounts to a contravention of the RCMP Code of Conduct and the subject member has left the Force before a conduct meeting or hearing could take place. Conduct measures taken against a member and the final written conduct decision, will be made public through a searchable web-based database. Conduct measures taken against a member following a complaint of harassment may be disclosed to the complainant in the case of a founded complaint. This information may also be used for research, planning, evaluation and audit and statistical purposes and may be matched with personal information described in the following information banks: RCMP PPU 070 (Applicants and Cadets Records); RCMP PPU 090 (Honours and Awards); RCMP PPE 801 (RCMP Member Performance Review and Appraisal Records); RCMP PPE 802 (RCMP Member Service Records); RCMP PPE 803 (RCMP Member Promotion Board Proceedings Records); RCMP PPE 804 (RCMP Member Grievance Records); RCMP PPE 806 (RCMP Member's Pay and Allowance Records); RCMP PPE 815 (RCMP Member Conflict of Interest and Post Employment Code Records); and RCMP PPE 818 (Employment Equity Program); RCMP PPU 085 (Complaints Against the RCMP or a Member, Enquiries and General Assistance).

**Retention and Disposal Standards:** Information in this bank is retained by the RCMP for a minimum of 10 years. If the file has been designated as having enduring value it will be transferred to the control of Library and Archives Canada, otherwise it will be retained by the RCMP until it no longer has business value. At that point, it will be destroyed.

**RDA Number:** 2000/030

**Related Record Number:** RCMP EMP 001

**TBS Registration:** 001017

**Bank Number:** RCMP PPE 805

## Occupational Health Records of RCMP Members and Other Persons Applying to Serve in Foreign Policing Responsibilities

**Description:** This bank contains personal information on regular and civilian members, special constable members of the RCMP for the purpose of administering occupational health services, health care benefits and disability management and accommodation. Information in this bank provides an overview of relevant medical and psychological information to support an assessment of a member's current health, fitness for duty and functional capacity in the workplace and to facilitate access to health care benefits. Occupational health records may contain treating health professional's reports, prescription medicine details, laboratory tests and reports, immunization records, treatment dates, hospital records, or other treatment records, work-related illness or injury claims, dental services, psychological testing, physical fitness testing, and fitness for duty assessments. Workplace hazardous exposure and incidents reports may also be maintained. It may also contain information on the medical condition of a dependant when a foreign or isolated posting is under consideration. In the past, a member's social insurance number may have been included on medical records/documents received from the Department of National Defence Canadian Forces Medical Services on members who received medical treatment/services from that department. In addition to the requirements indicated on the Personal Information Request Form, individuals must provide their full name and regimental, public service or badge number. Serving members must indicate the location of the material they wish to access. Individuals wishing to access only specific information should identify the material desired to expedite the processing of their requests. Medical information may also be included in the Personal Information Index under the Department of Veteran Affairs Members have a right of access to their personal information contained in their occupational health file. The access can be through a formal request under the Privacy Act or through an informal written request made directly to Occupational Health Services. For retired members, the request can also be submitted to National Headquarters Records Services or Archives Management Section where the file is held once the member is struck off strength.

**Class of Individuals:** Serving and former regular and civilian members, special constable members of the RCMP, and persons who applied and/or served in foreign policing responsibilities.

**Purpose:** The information is collected to support the internal administration of the RCMP Occupational Health Services program(s); the RCMP Disability Management and Accommodation Program; and the RCMP Health Care Entitlements and Benefits Program.

With respect to regular members, special constables, civilian members, to assess suitability to perform duties and make recommendations for posting, promotion and continued service where their mental health or physical health condition is a determining factor: entitlement to sick leave; and eligibility for insurance coverage. Under the S&SP umbrella, the National DNA Data Bank (NDDB) provides criminal and humanitarian investigative support.

The NDDB is a national database of DNA profiles, used to identify, link and investigate individuals involved in designated offences, and to assist in the investigation of missing persons and unidentified human remains. The NDDB collects this information in accordance with the DNA Identification Act. The NDDB is comprised of seven different indices: the Convicted Offenders Index (COI), the Crime Scene Index (CSI), the Relatives of Missing Persons Index (RMI), the Missing Persons Index (MPI), the Human Remains Index (HRI), the Victims Index (VI), and the Voluntary Donors Index (VDI). S&SP also provides scientific DNA identification support services, where DNA profile information will not be entered into or compared with the NDDB national database indices.

Personal information collected and retained by the program is limited to that which is authorized by law and demonstrably necessary to support the investigation at hand, whether criminal or humanitarian in nature. Personal information collected may include name, contact information, sex, date of birth, biological sample, biometric information, DNA profile, court order information for convicted offenders (that is, description and nature of designated offence, court docket number, copy of the judicial order authorizing collection of DNA), declaration of consent and signature for volunteer donors.

In the operation and administration of the NDDB, the RCMP may collect information about select RCMP employees and contractors who have access to laboratory areas for quality assurance purposes. This may include name and DNA profile.

Personal information may be collected directly from an individual, in the case of a court order/authorization or volunteer donor, or indirectly, by extracting bodily substances off the body of the victim or deceased, or objects either found at the scene of a designated offence or from objects which belong to a missing person.

Note: Genetic information that is not related to a known individual is not considered to be personal information until such time as there is a serious possibility that the individual to whom it belongs may be identified.

**Class of Individuals:** Individuals convicted of a designated offence under the Criminal Code, individuals considered a victim of a designated offence, a volunteer, a missing person, or the relative of a missing person, select RCMP employees and contractors.

**Purpose:** Personal information is collected to support investigating authorities in identifying persons alleged to have committed designated offences (criminal purpose), and to find missing persons and identify human remains (humanitarian purpose), in accordance with section 3 of the DNA Identification Act.

**Consistent Uses:** Personal information described in this bank will only be used by the RCMP for the purposes for which it was first collected, or for a use consistent with that purpose.

The misuse or disclosure of DNA profiles under the DNA Identification Act carries penalties under the Act. S&SP only shares DNA information with other investigating authorities as permitted by legislation.

In limiting the use of DNA profiles contained in humanitarian indices, DNA profiles stored in the RMI cannot be compared to DNA profiles contained in criminal indices, including the COI, the CSI, the VI, and the VDI.

The DNA profiles of victims, volunteers, or missing persons may be compared to DNA profiles contained in the other indices of the NDDB. Matches, if found, can be used as evidence against the individual in a criminal investigation and/or in their subsequent prosecution. The information may also be used to prove that the individual is innocent of a designated offence or assist with humanitarian investigations.

Pursuant to subsection 6.4(1) of the DNA Identification Act, the RCMP may, on receipt of a DNA profile from the government of authorized institution of a foreign state, compare that profile with those already contained in the NDDB’s CSI, COI, MPI and HRI to determine whether there is a match between profiles. The results of any match may then be communicated to the investigating agency.

In accordance with the DNA Identification Act, the Commissioner may, on the request of a law enforcement agency in the course of the investigation of a designated offence, communicate a DNA profile contained in the CSI, MPI, or HRI to the government of a foreign state, an international organization established by the governments of states or an institution of any such government of international organization.

The sharing of DNA information contained within the NDDB with international organizations or governments is only allowed where the RCMP or Government of Canada has entered into an agreement or arrangement, in accordance with any regulations, with that government, international organization or institution, authorizing the communication solely for the purposes of, as the case may be, the investigation or prosecution of a criminal offence or the investigation of a missing person or human remains.

**Retention and Disposal Standards:** DNA profiles stored in the RMI, MPI, HRI, VI or VDI, along with the biological sample from which they were developed, will only be retained by the RCMP for so long as needed for an investigation, as prescribed by law, or in the humanitarian context, until a missing person has been located or human remains are identified.

Individuals may withdraw their consent at any time, upon which their DNA profile will be removed from the RMI and their sample destroyed without delay. Unless otherwise directed by the investigating authority, DNA profiles in the RMI and MPI will be automatically removed five years after their inclusion in the respective indices, and for relatives of missing persons the associated biological samples destroyed.

Individuals may withdraw their consent at any time, upon which their DNA profile will be removed from the VI and VDI without delay. Unless otherwise directed by the investigating authority, DNA profiles will be automatically removed five years after their inclusion in the VI or VDI.

For the COI and CSI, DNA profiles may be retained indefinitely. Subsection 9(1) of the DNA Identification Act requires information contained in the convicted offenders index to be kept in accordance with the Criminal Records Act and subject to the access removal requirements set out in subsection 9(2) and 9.1 of the DNA Identification Act. There is no requirement to destroy the DNA profiles of deceased offenders, since those profiles may be used to identify an individual associated with an open or unresolved investigation. Their bodily substances may however be destroyed when the RCMP Commissioner considers them to be no longer required or of any investigative value. Such DNA profiles will remain indefinitely in the NDDB unless removed in accordance with the DNA Identification Act.
