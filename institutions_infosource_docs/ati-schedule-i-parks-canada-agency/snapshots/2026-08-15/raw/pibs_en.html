Appendix — Personal information banks

Back to Info Source

Personal information banks (PIBs) are descriptions of personal information maintained by government institutions about individuals in support of specific programs and activities. The Privacy Act requires that personal information banks include all personal information that is organized and retrievable by an individual's name or by an identifying number, symbol or other particular assigned exclusively to that individual. Personal information banks must also include personal information that has been or is being used or is available for use for an administrative purpose.

Please consult the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat Glossary of Terms for descriptions of the various types of personal information banks and an explanation of their contents.

Below is a list of the Parks Canada Agency’s institution-specific PIBs.

Administration of marine activity permits

Description
This bank contains information on holders of marine activity permits issued under the Marine Activities in the Saguenay–St. Lawrence Marine Park Regulations. Personal information may include name, contact information, and other identification numbers.
Class of individuals
Owners or representatives of organizations.
Purpose
This bank serves as a registry of marine activity permits. Personal information is used for the administration of permits related to activities conducted in the marine park, including compliance monitoring and enforcement activities such as managing reports, complaints, notices of violation, and administrative follow-ups. The information is collected under section 8 of the Saguenay–St. Lawrence Marine Park Act.
Consistent uses
Personal information is shared with park wardens to ensure compliance with the Marine Activities in the Saguenay–St. Lawrence Marine Park Regulations and may be used for investigations, to issue notices of violation, or to suspend a permit. De-identified information may also be used for research, planning, evaluation, statistical purposes, and reporting.
Retention and disposal standards
Personal information will be retained for a minimum period of two (2) years following the last administrative action, except where the individual’s consent has been obtained and legislation permits immediate disposal. At the end of the retention period, records will be disposed of in accordance with the disposition authorities established by Library and Archives Canada.

Aquatic invasive species prevention permits

Description
This bank contains information used to confirm compliance with aquatic invasive species regulations. Personal information may include name, contact information, location of last use of watercraft and water gear, intended location of next use, watercraft, vessel and vehicle information such as type, model, registration and license plate.
Class of individuals
National parks visitors
Purpose
The personal information is used to administer the protection and conservation of natural and cultural resources and for the issuance of a watercraft use permit to comply with regulations related to the use of watercraft and water-related gear in the park. The information will also be used to record watercraft usage in the park and assess risk for introduction of aquatic invasive species. Personal information is collected pursuant to sections 8(2), 16(3) and 18 of Canada National Parks Act, the section 6 of Species at Risk Act and section 7 of the National Park General Regulations.
Consistent uses
The information will be used to assess risk of the introduction and spread of aquatic invasive species from the use of the visitor’s watercraft or water-related gear. Data will be aggregated and reported on annually in internal reports to inform management decisions. The personal information may also be shared with law enforcement branch for enforcing Canada National Parks Act and provision of various other statutes that support the Agency’s mandate.
Retention and disposal standards
Physical records will be retained for a maximum of 5 years and then they will be destroyed. Data analysis resulting from the records will not contain personal information.

Claims and Litigation Program

Description
This bank describes information collected, managed, and maintained by Parks Canada in connection with incidents involving property damage or personal injury that occur in places under its jurisdiction. This includes information provided by individuals (for example, park visitors, motorists) who submit a claim or report an incident involving Parks Canada’s infrastructure or property. Personal information collected may include name, contact information, driver’s license number and province of issue, vehicle information (make, model, color, year), license plate number and issuing province, insurance provider and policy details, description of the incident or claim, witness statements or supporting documentation, and photographs, video, or other recordings provided by claimants.
Class of individuals
Motorists, highway users, park visitors, and any individuals involved in or reporting a personal injury or property damage incident in Parks Canada’s administered places.
Purpose
Personal information is collected to document personal injury or property damage in anticipation of litigation and may be used by the Agency to make administrative decisions on claims by/against the Crown. The personal information is collected pursuant to section 8 (1) of the Canada National Parks Act and relevant Regulations found under the Act (such as the Highway Traffic Regulations) as well as the Crown Liability and Proceedings Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-50); decisions are made in accordance with Parks Canada Agency’s financial delegation instrument.
Consistent uses
This information will be used to determine liability in property damage or personal injury incidences, to approve settlement payment for losses or to resolve claims pursued by the Crown against third parties.
Retention and disposal standards
Personal information will be retained for a minimum of two (2) years after the last administrative action, except where individual consent has been provided and legislation allows for immediate disposal. The disposition standards set out by Library and Archives Canada will be followed after this retention period has ended.

Impact Assessment Environmental Surveillance Program

Description
This bank contains personal information collected and used to support the evaluation and monitoring of proposed actions, projects, or policies that may have environmental effects. Impact assessment is a key tool that enables Parks Canada to identify, mitigate, and manage potential adverse environmental impacts in support of its legislated mandate. As part of this process, Environmental Surveillance activities are conducted by Parks Canada surveillance officers to verify, during project construction / implementation that mitigation measures are implemented as required. The Parks Canada Impact Assessment Environmental Surveillance System is used to record observations made during these site visits. Personal information collected may include names, contact information, and identification numbers such as photographs and license plate numbers. This information is collected from individuals associated with project sites and is used to support follow-up, compliance verification and the implementation of any corrective actions required to minimize environmental impacts.
Class of individuals
On site Contractors, third-party proponents.
Purpose
The personal information is collected for the purpose of administering the Parks Canada Impact Assessment Program and supporting the protection and conservation of natural and cultural resources on lands administered by Parks Canada. Personal information is used to enable Parks Canada to meet its legal obligations under the Impact Assessment Act, including verifying compliance with applicable legislation, policies and conditions of project approvals, and to prevent adverse environmental effects. As part of this compliance function, Parks Canada surveillance officers may conduct inspections of project sites during the construction or implementation phases of approved projects to verify that required mitigation measures are implemented effectively. Personal information is collected pursuant to sections 8, 16 and 18 of the Canada National Parks Act and section 84 of the Impact Assessment Act.
Consistent uses
Personal information collected during Environmental Surveillance activities may be used for internal briefing and reporting related to Parks Canada’s Impact Assessment and Environmental Surveillance program. Personal information may also be disclosed to law enforcement branch for the purpose of enforcing Parks Canada legislation and responding to potential contraventions identified during the Environmental Surveillance activities.
Retention and disposal standards
Personal information will be retained for a minimum of two (2) years after the last administrative action, except where individual consent has been provided and legislation allows for immediate disposal. The disposition standards set out by Library and Archives Canada will be followed after this retention period has ended.

Law Enforcement Program

Description
This bank contains personal information about individuals that were implicated in an incident or investigation undertaken by Parks Canada Park wardens in enforcing the laws of Canada as they relate to the Agency mandate. The personal information collected as a result of an incident or investigation may include names; contact information; physical attributes; date of birth; vehicle information, vessel information, identification numbers; criminal history; company name; the date and nature of the alleged incident or investigation; and the law enforcement action (charged, fined, warned, evicted).
Class of individuals
Visitors and businesses within the jurisdiction of Parks Canada.
Purpose
Information collected as part of an incident or investigation may be used by the Agency for the purpose of enforcing the Canada National Parks Act and its regulations and other complementary legislation such as the Criminal Code, the Species at Risk Act, and other federal and provincial legislation for the purpose of protection and conservation of natural and cultural resources and protecting the quality of the visitor experience. Information collected as a result of an incident or investigation is entered into the Agency’s Incident and Event Management system for Law Enforcement (IEM-LE).
Consistent uses
Information within IEM-LE may be used to report critical incidents to senior management, produce year-end reports and to develop law enforcement policies or guidelines. Pursuant to section 8(2)(a), (e) and (f) of the Privacy Act, personal information may also be disclosed to an investigative body as listed in the Privacy Regulations or to a municipal, provincial/territorial, or other policing service, under certain circumstances as described in those sections of the Act.
Retention and disposal standards
Records will be retained for five years or until they no longer have operational value, after which they will be destroyed.

Visitor safety, human wildlife coexistence activities

Description
This bank describes personal information about individuals involved in incidents related to visitor safety and human–wildlife coexistence at Parks Canada administered places. The information is used to identify, manage, and respond to risks associated with visitor use, including prevention, incident response, coordination with emergency partners, and the management of interactions between visitors and wildlife through education, monitoring, and targeted management measures. Personal information may include an individual's name, contact information, emergency contact information, preferred language, date of birth, gender, identifying information related to their role in the incident (such as reporting person, witness, patient, or behavioural response), medical information (including injuries, illnesses, and allergies), vehicle or vessel information, licence and vehicle registration numbers, and insurance provider information.
Class of individuals
Visitors, witnesses, involved third parties, and first responders interacting with Parks Canada.
Purpose
Personal information is collected and used to assess, investigate, and respond to incidents affecting visitor safety, security, human–wildlife coexistence, and property at Parks Canada administered places. The information supports coordination with appropriate authorities, implementation of corrective actions, and trend analysis. Information collected as part of an incident or investigation is entered into the Agency's Incident and Event Management (IEM) system for Visitor Safety and Human–Wildlife Conflict. Personal information is collected pursuant to section 8 of the Canada National Parks Act, section 8 of the Parks Canada Agency Act, section 8 of the Canada National Marine Conservation Areas Act, section 8 of the Saguenay–St. Lawrence Marine Park Act, and section 5 of the Rouge National Urban Park Act.
Consistent uses
Personal information may be used to produce management and year-end reports, support research, report critical incidents to senior management, and develop policies and guidelines. Information may also be disclosed to law enforcement authorities for the administration and enforcement of the Canada National Parks Act and other legislation supporting Parks Canada's mandate. Where authorized by law, information may be shared with local authorities or the police of jurisdiction to support emergency response or continuity of care. Where necessary, personal information may also be shared with medical professionals or organizations providing emergency medical services, search and rescue partners, or insurance providers in relation to an incident.
Retention and disposal standards
Personal information will be retained for a minimum of two (2) years after the last administrative action, except where the individual has provided consent and applicable legislation permits earlier disposal. After the retention period has expired, the information will be disposed of in accordance with the disposition standards established by Library and Archives Canada.
